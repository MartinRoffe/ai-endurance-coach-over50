Five minutes before every ride

Non-negotiable

Cold tissue dropped straight into a fixed aggressive position is where a lot of shoulder pain begins. Cheapest item on this page.

  1. Two minutes easy spinning, sat upright, hands on the tops.
  2. Roll the shoulders back and down a few times; big slow arm circles, one side at a time.
  3. Three minutes easy, rotating through tops, hoods and drops for 30 seconds each so the tissue meets each position warm.
  4. Then settle in — soft bend in both elbows, and a deliberate hand-position change every five minutes after that.

BURN 1000 Calories With This KILLER KETTLEBELL Workout

Drop it

James Evans. Not "later" — off the list entirely, for two reasons.

The claim is fiction
  • A 30–45 min kettlebell circuit is realistically 350–500 kcal for a man your size.
  • Any title promising 1000 was built for the thumbnail, not for your programme.
It's aimed at the wrong target
  • It sells conditioning — the one thing you already have in abundance from cycling.

Buy one bell: a 32 kg

September · ~£78 new

It becomes your swing bell, your carry bell and eventually your heavy deadlift bell, and you'll still be using it in three years.

Why 32, not 28
  • The jump only applies to hip-driven, high-tolerance patterns where 33% is fine.
  • You keep 20 and 24 for higher-volume sets.
  • A 28 gets outgrown for swings within months, then sits as an orphan weight.
  • 28 is about £71 — you don't save anything either.
Everything else is conditional
  • Second 20 kg — only if your sessions actually call for pairs. Most over-40 follow-along content is single-bell throughout.
  • 36 or 40 kg — deadlifts and carries only. Not this year.
  • Adjustables — skip. Plates can shift during swings and each change takes 15–30 seconds. You have garage space, so there's no reason to accept that.

Buy secondhand if you're patient. Cast iron doesn't wear out — a scuffed 32 performs identically to a new one. Heavy bells turn up constantly on Marketplace and eBay because people buy them optimistically and then don't. Collection only; postage on 32 kg is punishing. One more thing to expect: cast iron bells get physically bigger as they get heavier, so the 32's handle is noticeably thicker than your 24's. Your grip will fail before your hips do on carries and long swing sets. That's normal, and it catches up.

Mobility

3×/week · 8–10 min

Ten minutes three times a week, done every week, beats an hour once. Do it after a ride or in the evening when tissue is warm — not as a cold pre-ride ritual. The hinge work here is doing double duty as your pike fix.

Single-leg RDL, light kettlebell

Hinge · hamstring

A loaded hamstring lengthener, and the drill most directly aimed at your failed pike. Transfers better than passive stretching because you're teaching the hip to hinge under load — which is what reaching the hoods actually is.

3 × 8 each leg16 kg3 s down, 1 s up
  1. Bell in one hand, stand on the opposite leg with a soft knee.
  2. Hinge from the hip, letting the free leg travel back as a counterweight. Chest and hips stay square to the floor.
  3. Lower slowly over three seconds until you feel a firm stretch high in the standing hamstring. Back stays long.
  4. Drive the standing hip forward to stand up. Pause a beat at the top.
Cues
  • Reach the heel backward, don't just tip forward.
  • Chase the stretch, not the depth.
  • Free hand on a wall is fine — this isn't a balance test.
Common mistakes
  • Hips opening to the side — kills the stretch.
  • Going heavy. It's a mobility drill in a strength costume.
  • Rounding the lower back at the bottom.

Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch

Pelvis

Twenty desk years plus a lot of pedalling shorten the hip flexors, which drag the pelvis into a tilt you then fight all ride. The posterior tilt is the exercise — without it you're just stretching your knee.

2 × 45 s each side
  1. Half-kneeling: back knee on something soft, front foot flat, both hips facing forward.
  2. Before moving anywhere, tuck your tailbone under and flatten your lower back. You should already feel the front of the back hip.
  3. Holding that tuck, squeeze the kneeling-side glute and drift your hips forward two or three centimetres. That's the whole range.
  4. Breathe out slowly, hold 45 s, swap. Add a same-side overhead reach to catch the upper attachment.
Hold

Foam roller thoracic extensions

Mid-back

You passed the wall test, so this is maintenance rather than repair — but it's what keeps that pass, and extension gained in the mid-back is extension your neck and arms don't have to manufacture.

10 slow reps3 roller positions
  1. Roller across your mid-back, perpendicular to the spine. Hands lightly behind the head, elbows narrow.
  2. Brace the abdomen so the movement can't leak into your lower back — this is the difference between the drill working and doing nothing.
  3. Exhale and drape your upper back backward over the roller. Small range. Pause two seconds at the end.
  4. Curl up, shift the roller two or three centimetres, repeat. Three positions between the bottom of the shoulder blades and the base of the neck.
Cues
  • Exhale into it; ribs stay down.
  • Segment by segment, not one big flop.
Common mistakes
  • Arching the lumbar spine — the usual way this becomes useless.
  • Pulling on the head.
  • Rolling on the lower back.

Doorway pec stretch — two heights

Anterior shoulder

Short pecs, and pec minor especially, tip the shoulder blade forward and down — precisely where your pain has been. Two arm heights because the fibres run in different directions.

2 × 30 s at 90°2 × 30 s at 120°Each side
  1. Forearm flat on a door frame, elbow at shoulder height (90°).
  2. Step the same-side foot through and rotate your chest away until you feel a broad stretch across the front of the shoulder.
  3. Keep the shoulder blade gently back and down — if the shoulder rolls forward you're stretching the joint capsule, not the muscle.
  4. Hold 30 s. Then raise the elbow to roughly 120°, above shoulder height, and repeat.
Hold

Trunk & shoulder block

After Strength 1 & 2 · 12 min

Low intensity, so it goes on riding days where it competes with nothing. Every hold ends at form failure, not at discomfort — a sagging plank trains the exact fault you're removing. Given the failed plank test, start at the bottom of each range.

Side plank

Highest value on this page

Trains the lateral trunk to stop your ribcage collapsing toward the bars and your pelvis dropping to one side. Directly aimed at the left-side pattern you started with.

3 × 30–45 s each sideBuild to 60 sElbow, not hand
  1. On your side, elbow directly under the shoulder, forearm forward, feet stacked or staggered.
  2. Lift the hips until shoulder, hip and ankle make one line. Push the floor away so you don't sag into the bottom shoulder.
  3. Squeeze the top glute and brace the abdominal wall. Head in line with the spine.
  4. Hold to the first sign of the hips dropping. Rest 30 s between sets.
Hold
Progression
  • Too hard: knees down, or top foot forward on the floor.
  • Too easy: top arm overhead, or lift the top leg.
  • Add load before adding time past 60 s.
Common mistakes
  • Rolling forward or back off the true side position.
  • Sinking into the supporting shoulder.
  • Holding your breath.

Bird-dog

Anti-extension

Teaches the trunk to stay quiet while the limbs move — which is what pedalling is, and the reason a stable trunk stops your shoulders taking the surplus.

3 × 8 each side3 s hold per rep
  1. Four-point kneeling, hands under shoulders, knees under hips. Flatten the lower back and brace.
  2. Extend the opposite arm and leg to horizontal — no higher. Hips stay level; imagine balancing a glass of water on your lower back.
  3. Hold three seconds, reaching long through fingertips and heel.
  4. Return slowly, tapping hand and knee down before the next rep. Alternate.
Cues
  • Reach long, don't lift high.
  • Push the floor away with the supporting hand.
Common mistakes
  • Hip hiking or rotating — the fault this exists to remove.
  • Arching as the leg rises.
  • Rushing. Speed hides instability.

Suitcase carry

Kettlebell days

Anti-lateral-flexion, loaded and upright — the closest thing off the bike to resisting a torso lean. You're already at your load ceiling here with the 24, which is the strongest single reason to buy the 32.

3 × 30 m each side24 kg now, 32 kg from OctoberExtra set on the right
  1. One bell in one hand, arm hanging. Stand tall, shoulders level, ribs stacked over pelvis.
  2. Set the shoulder before moving: pull it slightly back and down so the weight can't drag it forward.
  3. Walk 30 m with deliberate, normal-length steps. Do not lean away from the bell.
  4. Set down, swap hands, repeat. If your torso tips, it's too heavy for the purpose.
Cues
  • Walk as if carrying nothing.
  • Brace the side opposite the bell.
  • Extra set on the right, given the left-side history.
Common mistakes
  • Side-bending toward or away from the load.
  • Shrugging the loaded shoulder.
  • Shortening the stride to cope.

Wall slides with lift-off

Serratus

Trains the serratus anterior to hold the shoulder blade flat against the ribcage. When it doesn't, the front of the shoulder takes the load — your symptom.

2 × 12Slow, no momentum
  1. Face a wall, forearms on it, elbows about shoulder width and roughly chest height.
  2. Brace the abdomen and tuck the ribs down so you can't arch to gain range.
  3. Slide the forearms up, actively reaching long — push the wall away and let the shoulder blades travel up and around the ribcage.
  4. At the top of your comfortable range, lift the forearms a centimetre off the wall for one second, then replace and slide down slowly.
Cues
  • Reach, don't shrug.
  • Ribs stay down the whole set.
Common mistakes
  • Arching the lower back to get higher.
  • Elbows flaring wide.
  • Going above the point where the shoulder pinches — stop below it.

Band face pulls, or prone Y / T raises

Posterior shoulder

The counterweight to hours in a closed, forward position, and the balancing entry to all the pressing and carrying elsewhere in the plan.

2 × 15Light band2 s hold
  1. Face pulls: band anchored at head height, one end in each hand, thumbs pointing back.
  2. Pull toward your forehead, leading with the elbows, finishing wide with hands either side of your head.
  3. Squeeze two seconds — shoulder blades together and slightly down, ribs down — then return slowly.
  4. No band? Face down on the floor: arms in a Y (2 × 12), then a T (2 × 12), thumbs up, two seconds at the top.
Cues
  • Elbows lead, hands follow.
  • Chin tucked; neck stays out of it.
Common mistakes
  • Band too heavy, so traps and lower back take over.
  • Head poking forward.
  • Ripping through reps.

Single-leg block

Bolted onto Monday · 8 min

Bodyweight only. This is the gap kettlebell circuits leave: cycling is bilateral, fixed-plane and seated, so unilateral loading is both underdeveloped and directly transferable. Add load only once all three are clean at bodyweight.

Split squat

Bodyweight → 16 kg

The single-leg squat pattern, without the balance demand of a lunge. Exposes side-to-side differences that a two-legged squat hides completely.

3 × 8 each legSlow down, controlled up
  1. Split stance, feet about a stride apart, torso upright. Back heel lifted.
  2. Drop straight down — back knee toward the floor, not forward — until the back knee is just above the ground.
  3. Front shin stays close to vertical and the front knee tracks over the middle of the foot.
  4. Drive up through the front foot. Reset your stance each rep rather than drifting.
Cues
  • Down, not forward.
  • Fingertips on a wall for balance is fine.
  • Note which side is harder — that's your weaker leg, and it's information worth having.
Common mistakes
  • Stance too short, which turns it into a knee-dominant grind.
  • Torso pitching forward.
  • Adding weight before both sides are equal.

Step-down

Eccentric control

Trains the slow lowering that cycling never asks for. Good for knee resilience and directly relevant to controlling a single-leg position under fatigue.

2 × 8 each leg3 s lowering20–30 cm step
  1. Stand on a step on one leg, other leg hanging free just off the edge.
  2. Lower yourself over three seconds by bending the standing knee, tapping the free heel lightly on the floor.
  3. Keep the hips level throughout — no dropping toward the free side.
  4. Push back up through the standing leg. If the hip drops, use a lower step.
Cues
  • Tap, don't land.
  • Standing knee tracks over the foot.
Common mistakes
  • Dropping fast and bouncing out of the bottom.
  • Pelvis tilting — the fault to watch for given your history.
  • Step too high, too soon.

Single-leg glute bridge

Posterior chain

Glute work in hip extension without loading the spine. Cyclists are chronically quad-dominant, and a glute that fires properly takes work off the lower back and stabilises the pelvis on the saddle.

2 × 10 each side2 s squeeze at the top
  1. On your back, one foot flat and close to your backside, the other knee pulled toward your chest.
  2. Tuck the pelvis slightly, then drive through the planted heel to lift the hips.
  3. Stop where your hips are level and your lower back is not arching — that's the top, wherever it happens to be.
  4. Squeeze the glute for two seconds, lower slowly. Both hips stay square to the ceiling.
Cues
  • Push the floor away with the heel.
  • If you feel it in the hamstring, move the foot closer in.
Common mistakes
  • Arching the back to gain height.
  • One hip dropping or rotating.
  • Feeling it entirely in the lower back — that means the glute isn't doing it.

Stop adjusting, get seen

When this stops being a fit problem

  • Numbness that persists for hours after you get off the bike.
  • Any loss of grip strength or clumsiness in the hand.
  • Symptoms that wake you at night.
  • Shoulder pain appearing off the bike, or a painful arc lifting your arm to the side.

Those point to nerve entrapment or a shoulder structure rather than weight distribution, and no amount of stem swapping or planking will touch them. Physio assessment first, then come back to the plan.

At 50 the recovery side stops being optional garnish — sleep and adequate protein will shape the next six months more than exercise selection will. And keep changing one thing at a time: if you alter the saddle, the stem and the programme in the same week, you'll learn nothing about any of them. Two numbers once a month will tell you whether the position or the body is still the limiter.
