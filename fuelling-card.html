The one thing that actually has to happen the night before

Fill two bottles and count the pocket food onto the counter. That's five minutes, not an evening. For the three big days (16, 22, 24 Aug) also fill two 60 g powder bags for the resupply stop. This is the entire prep burden for the camp — everything else is bought.

Rides over ~5 hours — you cannot carry it all

Six 750 ml carb bottles do not fit on a bike. Anything past ~5 hours has to be fuelled with a resupply plan, not a packing list. Decide the stops before you leave.
  • Carry: 2 bottles (1 carb, 1 electrolyte) + ~6 items of pocket food ≈ first 3 hours at 90 g/h.
  • Stop 1 (~3h): refill both bottles, add carb powder from a pre-measured zip bag. Eat something solid off the bike.
  • Stop 2 (~5h30): same again. On the charity ride, the organised feed stops are these — use them properly rather than grabbing one item and rolling on.
  • Pre-measure the powder: 60 g per zip bag, one bag per planned refill, labelled. This is the single change that would have prevented 2 Aug.
  • Shop stops: Coke 330 ml = 35 g, a pastry = 40–50 g. Both count. One Lucozade does not.

Charity ride — two-day specifics

Day 1 (190 km) is 30–40% longer than anything you have ridden

  • Two days of carb loading beforehand — 8–10 g/kg on 11 and 12 Sep.
  • First 3 hours strictly under the Z2 ceiling. Bank no early fatigue.
  • Fuel to the clock, not to appetite. Appetite disappears before the legs do.
  • Target ≥ 700 g carbs across the day. If you are under 400 g at the 4-hour mark, you are already behind — do not try to catch up in one hit; add a bottle and hold the pace down.

Day 2 (120 km) on tired legs

  • Recovery inside 30 min of Day 1 finishing: 60–80 g carbs + 20–30 g protein, then a full evening meal.
  • Start very easy — the legs come good after 45–60 min if they are fed.
  • Same 90 g/h from minute 0. Glycogen is not fully restored overnight, so on-bike carbs matter more on Day 2, not less.
